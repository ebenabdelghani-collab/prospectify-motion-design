# Lessons — retention-style reel (founder reference, 2026-10-06)

Source: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DYU9ZblO5D_/
- 34 s, 1080×1920, 30 fps. A creator talks to camera, with graphics that illustrate every sentence.
- The file was downloaded once for frame analysis (4 fps contact sheets), then deleted. Nothing from it is reused.

**Rule for every future Prospectify motion piece:** apply these principles. They are about structure and editing, not looks.

## What makes it hold attention from A to Z
1. **The proof comes first, in the first frame.**
   - It opens on a "before" graph (479 views, retention falling off a cliff) next to a promise ("stupidly simple changes").
   - At about 3.7 s it flips to the "after" graph (1,127,928 views).
   - The payoff is shown before it is explained.
2. **Every sentence has a picture.**
   - Each claim is illustrated by a real-looking UI card floating above the speaker: an analytics graph, a music library, an audio waveform, a list of links, the retention screen.
   - Nothing is said without being shown.
3. **Word-by-word captions in the centre of the screen.**
   - 1 to 4 words at a time, sentence case, white, with **one key word in a highlight colour** (yellow / orange: "risers", "anticipation", "longer", "#3 comes in").
   - They are always readable with the sound off.
4. **A numbered structure (#1, #2, #3) creates open loops.**
   - "#2 comes in" and "#3 comes in" promise more and keep people watching to the end.
5. **One big typographic shock per section.**
   - The 3D red word stack "UNORIGINAL / BORING" lands on the beat while the speaker pauses.
   - It is used once, as a punctuation mark, not everywhere.
6. **Annotation instead of explanation.**
   - A red circle draws onto the graph at the exact moment the drop is mentioned ("90% of you guys do #1").
7. **Constant but small motion.**
   - Cards pop in with a slight scale and blur, then hold.
   - The camera keeps reframing (push-ins, a wide-to-close change near the end).
   - No hard "slide" transitions; the speaker never leaves the frame.
8. **Show the thing you're talking about while it happens.**
   - When he says "use risers", a riser waveform card appears and the riser actually plays.
   - Sound and picture prove each other.
9. **A fast cadence.**
   - A new visual element or caption change every 0.25–0.5 s.
   - The longest static hold is about 1.5 s.
10. **The loop ending.** "Just like what I did right now": the video itself demonstrates the tip, which feels clever and rewatchable.

## How to apply it to Prospectify (checklist)
- [ ] **Open on the result or contrast** (e.g. a lead card with a 94 score plus "found in 3 seconds" vs the 2 AM spreadsheet) within the first 1–2 s, before any explanation.
- [ ] **Word-by-word centre captions** throughout, with one accent word per phrase in the Prospectify coral.
- [ ] **A numbered structure** when it fits (#1 find, #2 why, #3 message, #4 prompt).
- [ ] **One UI card per sentence**, floating and annotated live (circles, underlines, arrows drawn on the beat).
- [ ] **One big typographic punch per act**, used sparingly.
- [ ] **The sound demonstrates the picture**: whoosh with the card, impact with the punch word, riser before each reveal.
- [ ] **No static hold longer than about 1.5 s**, and something changes every 0.25–0.5 s.
- [ ] **A human presence** if possible (the founder talking to camera with UI overlays). It is the strongest retention format in this reference. It needs footage from the founder.
- [ ] **An end that loops back to the opening** promise.
